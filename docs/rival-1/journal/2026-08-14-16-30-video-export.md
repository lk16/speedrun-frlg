# Recording a finished run: `frlg video`

The project could prove a run and could not show one. `frlg video` closes that: one command that
replays a committed run and writes a video plus the title and description to publish it with, into
a dated folder under `ignored/videos/` (named like these journal entries).

## The gate is the point

A video is the only artifact that leaves the project, it is watched by people who will never open
the ledger, and it cannot be corrected after upload. So the command refuses unless

- every segment's `tier2` field says `passed` -- tier 1 alone is the sandbox's emulator agreeing
  with itself, which is not acceptance; and
- the ledger and every log it names are tracked and unmodified in git.

The description links the commit that *introduced* the tier-2 verdict, found by walking the
ledger's own history newest-first and keeping the last commit whose copy still carries exactly
these verdicts. Linking HEAD would have pointed a viewer at whatever landed afterwards.

## Sound was the interesting part

The shim had no audio at all. mGBA 0.11 hands audio out as an `mAudioBuffer` of interleaved stereo
int16 (`getAudioBuffer`), filled by the core's own sample event, and it *drops* what does not fit
its 0x4000-frame ring -- so a capture has to drain it every frame. Four flat entry points on the
shim (`frlg_audio_sample_rate`, `_channels`, `_read`, `_clear`) and it works: peak 19200 within 900
frames of boot, so it is real audio and not silence.

The trap: **the sample rate is not constant.** The GBA derives it from SOUNDBIAS's resolution
field, and LeafGreen moves between 32768 and 65536 Hz three times during the boot. Writing one
fixed-rate stream and ignoring that would have put the sound ~1.6 s ahead of the picture for the
rest of the run. So each frame's chunk is laid onto the *video's* clock -- frame `i` ends at
`round((i+1) * rate * 280896 / 16777216)` output samples, nearest-neighbour within the chunk --
which puts sound and picture back in step at every single frame, whatever the core did in between.
Two unit tests pin that, including the 32768 -> 65536 change.

## The codec guess was backwards

FFV1 in Matroska was the obvious archival choice, and it is 3-4x *worse* here: 6.6 MB against 2.2
MB for 1020 frames. FFV1 is intra-only, and a GBA screen mostly does not move -- a battle text box
costs a full frame every frame. Lossless H.264 in RGB (`libx264rgb -qp 0`) has inter-frame
prediction and takes the still stretches almost for free. Both were checked rather than trusted:
encoded from a raw stream, decoded back, `cmp` clean, and ALAC likewise at 65536 Hz. So the default
is mp4 (H.264 RGB + ALAC), with mkv (FFV1 + FLAC) kept as the archival option.

rival-1: 9658 frames, 960x640, 32 MiB, about a minute to make.

The upscale is `--scale 4`, nearest-neighbour, so every output pixel is a copy of exactly one input
pixel and the file is still reversible to the console's own frame -- but a video site will not
spend bitrate on a 240-line source.

## Cost

Two replays plus the encode. ffmpeg wants its audio input openable at start-up and the raw picture
is ~6 GB for the brock run, far too much to spool, so the deterministic replay is done twice --
once for sound, once to pipe the picture in -- rather than buffering either.

ffmpeg is host-only, like tier 2: it is not in the sandbox image and cannot be installed there.
`docs/sandbox.md` and `docs/harness.md` say so.
